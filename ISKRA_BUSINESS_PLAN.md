# Project ISKRA Business and Growth Plan

## Executive Summary

Project ISKRA should become Montreal's leading **real-life social nightlife brand**: curated events where guests use a QR-based web experience to discover people in the same venue, express interest privately, match, chat, and find one another through approximate venue guidance.

ISKRA is not positioned as another dating app. Its promise is:

> Better nights, easier introductions, real connections.

The business has two connected products:

1. **ISKRA Events:** branded nightlife experiences that generate ticket, VIP, sponsor, and merchandise revenue.
2. **ISKRA Venue Platform:** the QR check-in, temporary profiles, matching, group discovery, ticketing, scanner, CRM, and analytics system used at ISKRA events and eventually licensed to partner venues.

Montreal is a strong launch market. The city explicitly treats nightlife as part of its cultural identity and is investing in safer, structured nighttime activity. Tourisme Montreal reported 11.9 million overnight visitors in 2025, 7.3% more than in 2024, creating an additional visitor segment beyond local residents.

## Strategic Goal

Build a recognizable Montreal brand with:

- 10,000 qualified local followers within 12 months.
- 5,000 opted-in email/SMS contacts within 12 months.
- 1,500 registered ISKRA web accounts within 6 months of app launch.
- 300-500 attendees at monthly flagship events by month 9.
- At least three recurring venue partners by month 12.
- At least 30% of ticket buyers returning to another ISKRA event within 90 days.

These are operating targets, not forecasts. They should be adjusted after the first three events provide real conversion and retention data.

## Market Position

### Primary Customers

- Singles aged 21-35 who dislike conventional dating apps.
- Newcomers, international students, tourists, and young professionals who want to meet people locally.
- Friend groups seeking structured but non-awkward social experiences.
- Nightlife guests who are open to dating, friendship, dancing, or group connections.

### Business Customers

- Nightclubs and bars that want higher attendance and repeat visits.
- Festivals, university associations, hotels, and cultural organizations.
- Beverage, fashion, beauty, rideshare, hospitality, and lifestyle sponsors.

### Competitive Difference

ISKRA should own the category **"the social layer of the room."** Dating apps optimize for prolonged online use. ISKRA should optimize for guests putting down their phones and meeting in person.

The defensible advantage is the complete system:

- A recognizable event brand.
- A venue-specific temporary social network.
- Ticketing, QR entry, matching, groups, and privacy controls.
- First-party audience data collected with consent.
- Venue analytics and repeat-event marketing.
- A growing network of participating venues and ambassadors.

## Revenue Model

### Initial Revenue

1. **General admission:** target CAD $20-$30 depending on venue and talent.
2. **VIP/social fast-pass:** CAD $15-$30 upsell for priority entry, welcome drink, hosted introductions, or reserved areas.
3. **Group packages:** four or six tickets sold together at a modest discount.
4. **Brand sponsorships:** sponsored photo areas, welcome drinks, match moments, prizes, or content.
5. **Venue revenue share:** negotiate a percentage of ticket or bar revenue when ISKRA supplies the audience and promotion.

### Later Revenue

- Monthly venue-platform license.
- Per-event software and scanner fee.
- White-label event platform for hospitality groups.
- Optional consumer membership with early access, lower ticket fees, and member-only events.
- Merchandise and limited collaborations.
- Corporate social mixers and private events.

Do not charge users to message a mutual match during an event. The core connection loop must feel generous and work well before premium features are introduced.

## Customer Acquisition Engine

### 1. The Event-to-Website Growth Loop

Every event should create the next event's audience:

1. A guest sees creator content, a partner post, or a friend's referral.
2. The guest visits a dedicated event page and buys a ticket.
3. At the venue, QR codes activate the temporary ISKRA experience.
4. The guest meets people and receives a post-event recap.
5. The account stores tickets and preferences, not expired private event content.
6. The guest receives early access and a personal referral link for the next event.
7. Referral activity generates new attendees at a lower acquisition cost.

### 2. Short-Form Content

Publish five recurring content series instead of random flyers:

- **The Spark:** short interviews asking Montrealers what creates chemistry.
- **Tonight's Signal:** DJ, venue, dress code, and event-vibe previews.
- **Meet-Cute Stories:** consented stories from people who met at ISKRA.
- **Montreal After Dark:** recommendations and collaborations with local venues.
- **Behind the Room:** setup, safety team, QR system, lighting, and event production.

Target 4-6 short videos per week across Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Each video needs one action: join the waitlist, vote on the next theme, or buy a ticket.

### 3. Micro-Creator Network

Recruit 20-30 Montreal creators with approximately 3,000-50,000 followers in nightlife, dating, fashion, music, student life, tourism, and newcomer communities.

Offer:

- A unique tracking link and promo code.
- Complimentary entry after a minimum sales threshold.
- 10%-15% ticket commission or a fixed content fee.
- A creator-hosted zone or mini-event.
- Monthly performance reporting so strong partners receive larger campaigns.

Avoid paying large creators before attribution is working. Optimize for ticket sales, registrations, and high-quality attendees rather than views alone.

### 4. Ambassador Program

Build small, accountable ambassador teams across universities, coworking spaces, gyms, hospitality staff, newcomer groups, and cultural communities.

Ambassadors receive:

- A personal referral dashboard.
- One free ticket after five paid referrals.
- VIP access after ten referrals.
- Commission or event-host status after twenty referrals.
- Monthly creative assets in English, French, Ukrainian, and Russian where appropriate.

### 5. Venue Partnerships

Pitch venues on measurable outcomes rather than technology:

- Incremental attendance on a normally weaker night.
- Prepaid tickets and a known arrival curve.
- Better guest engagement and repeat-visit data.
- Anonymous zone/activity analytics.
- Branded content from the event.
- Consent-first safety and moderation tools.

Start with one consistent home venue, then run partner popups. Consistency builds the brand; popups expand reach.

### 6. Local Partnerships

Create reciprocal promotions with:

- Boutique hotels and hostels.
- Language schools and newcomer organizations.
- Gyms, dance studios, salons, and fashion retailers.
- Restaurants offering an ISKRA pre-party menu.
- Rideshare/taxi partners and late-night food businesses.
- University graduate associations and professional networks.

The partner should receive a code, branded landing page, and transparent result report.

### 7. Paid Media

Use paid advertising only after organic creative proves it can convert.

- Begin with CAD $50-$100 per day for 10-14 days around each event.
- Run separate campaigns for video views, landing-page visits, and ticket conversion.
- Retarget site visitors, video viewers, abandoned checkouts, and previous buyers where valid consent exists.
- Rotate at least five creative concepts per event.
- Stop ads based on cost per ticket, not engagement rate.

### 8. Referral and Scarcity Mechanics

- Give each registered user a referral link.
- Offer a two-person "bring your spark" ticket.
- Unlock small rewards at 3, 5, and 10 referrals.
- Show real ticket tiers and capacity, never fake countdowns.
- Open member presale 48 hours before public sales.
- Release a limited last-call ticket tier instead of constant discounts.

## Event Portfolio

### Monthly Flagship

**ISKRA: Signal Night**  
The full branded experience: major DJ, visual production, QR social app, match compass, photographers, hosted introductions, and sponsor activation.

Purpose: revenue, brand awareness, hero content, and audience growth.

### Recurring Event Ideas

1. **Red Light / Green Light**  
Guests select a digital intention: dating, friends, dancing, networking, or just enjoying the music. Lighting and wristband alternatives support conversation without publicly exposing private preferences.

2. **The 11:11 Match**  
At 11:11 p.m., the app opens a coordinated mutual-introduction round. Matches receive a shared visual signal and a suggested venue zone.

3. **Secret Location Spark**  
Ticket holders receive the venue in stages. Use only after ISKRA has enough trust and a strong safety/transport plan.

4. **New in Montreal**  
A hosted social night for newcomers, international professionals, students, and travellers. Include conversation hosts and multilingual onboarding.

5. **Friends First**  
Focus on friendship and group formation rather than dating. This broadens the audience and creates a lower-pressure entry into the brand.

6. **After-Work Chemistry**  
An early-evening event for professionals, with structured introductions from 7-9 p.m. and a dance program afterward.

7. **Vinyl and Velvet**  
Smaller premium event with vinyl DJs, cocktail pairing, editorial dress code, and limited capacity.

8. **Neon Masquerade**  
Guests reveal more profile details through stages during the night. Avoid physical masks where they interfere with door identification or safety.

9. **Dance Card**  
Dance instructors run short, optional beginner sessions before the DJ set. The app helps guests find partners or small groups.

10. **Taste and Tempo**  
Restaurant pre-party followed by a club event. Sell dinner-plus-entry packages with a hospitality partner.

11. **Creator Takeover**  
A proven micro-creator curates music, dress direction, and guest list while ISKRA operates ticketing and the social platform.

12. **City Exchange**  
Montreal guests meet a visiting community from Toronto, Ottawa, New York, or another partner city. Later, run a reciprocal event there.

13. **No-Alcohol Spark**  
Premium zero-proof cocktails, music, games, and social discovery. This demonstrates responsible nightlife and reaches an underserved audience.

14. **Queer Spark**  
Designed with local LGBTQ+ hosts and organizations, with tailored discovery controls and safety policies rather than merely changing the flyer.

15. **Sunday Reset Social**  
Daytime coffee, wellness, music, and friendship-oriented group discovery. It keeps the brand active outside nightclub hours.

### Recommended Cadence

- One flagship ticketed night each month.
- One smaller themed social or partner event every two weeks.
- One community/creator collaboration per month.
- One members-only experiment per quarter.

Do not launch all formats at once. Start with Signal Night, New in Montreal, Friends First, and After-Work Chemistry; compare acquisition cost, attendance, app activation, safety incidents, and repeat purchase.

## Website and Product Conversion Plan

### Landing Page

The home page should answer within five seconds:

- What is ISKRA?
- When and where is the next event?
- Who is it for?
- Why is it different?
- What should the visitor do next?

Primary action: **Get tickets**.  
Secondary action: **Join the next drop**.

### Required Conversion Features

- Dedicated SEO/shareable page for every event.
- Fast Stripe checkout with Apple Pay/Google Pay where available.
- Group-ticket and referral options.
- Abandoned-checkout recovery with valid consent.
- Account area containing tickets, referrals, events, and preferences.
- Real scarcity based on ticket inventory.
- UTM, creator-code, and referral attribution.
- Multilingual event details where campaigns target those communities.
- Post-event survey and referral prompt.
- A/B testing for headlines, hero media, pricing tiers, and checkout steps.

### On-Site Event Activation

- Place QR markers at entry, bar, dance floor, patio, and quieter social areas.
- Give staff a 30-second explanation script.
- Show privacy and consent information before location/profile activation.
- Use screens or a host announcement to demonstrate the app early in the night.
- Reward activation with a useful benefit, such as a welcome draw, photo access, or event-only offer.

## 90-Day Launch Plan

### Days 1-30: Foundation

- Interview 30 target customers and 10 venue operators.
- Confirm one home venue and two backup venues.
- Create the creator and ambassador tracking system.
- Finish registration, ticketing, referral, analytics, moderation, and event-admin flows.
- Publish the safety code, privacy summary, refund policy, and community standards.
- Produce 20 reusable short-form videos before ticket sales open.
- Launch a waitlist with a goal of 750 qualified Montreal contacts.

### Days 31-60: First Growth Campaign

- Announce the first Signal Night four weeks in advance.
- Activate 10 creators and 15 ambassadors.
- Run one low-cost preview social to test onboarding and QR placement.
- Start retargeting after enough qualified traffic exists.
- Secure one event sponsor and three cross-promotion partners.
- Target 200-250 paid attendees for the first full event.

### Days 61-90: Retention

- Send recap content within 24 hours.
- Survey attendees within 12 hours while the experience is fresh.
- Open the next event presale within 48 hours.
- Segment buyers, app users, no-shows, creators, and referrals.
- Run the second event using the best-performing creative and channel mix.
- Publish a venue case study showing attendance, app activation, and return intent without exposing personal data.

### Initial Funnel Targets

Use these only as test thresholds:

- Landing page to email waitlist: 8%-15%.
- Landing page to account: 5%-10%.
- Event page to purchase: 3%-8% from cold traffic; higher from referrals.
- Ticket buyer to venue app activation: 50%+.
- Attendee to next-event presale: 10%-20%.
- Referred share of sales by month 6: 20%+.

## Safety, Privacy, and Trust

Trust is a growth strategy, not just a legal task.

- Require 18+ confirmation and venue ID verification at the door.
- Keep exact location unavailable; use broad venue zones.
- Require mutual consent before compass/location sharing.
- Provide block, report, stop-sharing, and staff escalation controls.
- Make scanner access separate from the admin system.
- Delete temporary profiles, chats, and media on the promised schedule.
- Appoint a privacy lead and publish their contact details.
- Conduct a privacy impact assessment before expanding location, fingerprinting, behavioural profiling, or cross-border processing.
- Collect marketing consent separately from account/ticket consent.
- Never buy or scrape email lists.
- Maintain trained security, sober event leadership, incident logs, water, zero-proof options, and safe-ride information.

Quebec Law 25 requires clear consent by purpose and prior notice when identification, location, or profiling technology is used; such functionality cannot simply be activated by default. Canadian e-marketing guidance also requires clear consent and a working unsubscribe mechanism. Venue partners must confirm that their alcohol, dance, entertainment, capacity, and operating permissions cover each event.

## Team for the First Year

- Founder/brand and partnership lead.
- Event producer/operations lead.
- Growth/content manager.
- Part-time community and ambassador manager.
- Technical product lead.
- Contract photographer/video editor.
- Trained event safety lead and venue security.
- Privacy/legal/accounting advisors as needed.

Keep the fixed team small. Scale hosts, security, creators, and production contractors per event until demand is predictable.

## Recommended Strategic Decision

For the next six months, focus on one promise and one city:

> ISKRA is the best night in Montreal for meeting people naturally.

Do not market every website feature. Market the outcome: a memorable night, a comfortable reason to approach someone, and a real chance to connect. The software should make that promise work; the event should make people talk about it afterward.
